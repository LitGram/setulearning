# English Chapter 9: My Mother at Sixty-Six (Kamala Das) — Smart Study Guide

**Subject:** English Compulsory | **Code:** 02  
**Class:** 12th RBSE / NCERT  
**Textbook:** Flamingo (Poetry - Chapter 9)  
**Marks Weightage:** 3 to 4 Marks (Stanza Explanation with Reference to Context)  

---

## 🌟 1. Overview & Strategy

> **Teacher's Direct Advice:**  
> Kamala Das's poignant confessional poem about filial anxiety and the inevitability of ageing and separation:  
> 1. **The Ash-like Face:** Driving to Cochin airport, the poet notices her mother dozing with mouth open, her face ashen like that of a corpse (Simile: *corpse-like*).  
> 2. **The Contrast (Vibrant Life):** To drive away the painful thought, she looks out at "Young Trees sprinting" and "merry children spilling out of their homes" (symbols of vibrant youth, vitality, and continuous life).  
> 3. **Late Winter's Moon:** At airport security check, mother looks wan and pale as a "late winter's moon" (dim, obscure, losing vitality).  
> 4. **Parting Words & Masking Smile:** *"See you soon, Amma"* and all she did was *"smile and smile and smile..."* (an attempt to conceal her childhood ache/fear of losing her mother behind a reassuring smile).

---

## 📝 4. Solved Board Models (Stanza Explanation)

### Model 1: Explain with reference to context:
> *"I looked again at her, wan, pale*  
> *as a late winter's moon and felt that old*  
> *familiar ache, my childhood's fear,*  
> *but all I said was, see you soon, Amma,*  
> *all I did was smile and smile and smile..."* (4 Marks)

**Answer:**  
1. **Reference:** These lines are taken from the poem 'My Mother at Sixty-Six' composed by celebrated Indian-English poetess Kamala Das. (1 Mark)  
2. **Context:** The poet is at the Cochin airport security check, looking at her ageing mother standing a few yards away before departure. (1 Mark)  
3. **Explanation:** The poet compares her mother's wan, colorless face to the late winter's moon, which is hazy, obscure, and lacks warmth and shine. Looking at her, the poet experiences her familiar childhood agony—the dread of separation and death of her mother. However, hiding her grief and anxiety, she cheerfully says farewell with "See you soon, Amma" and wears a lingering, repetitive smile to reassure both her mother and herself. (1.5 Marks)  
4. **Poetic Devices:** Simile (*as a late winter's moon*); Repetition (*smile and smile and smile*). (0.5 Mark)
