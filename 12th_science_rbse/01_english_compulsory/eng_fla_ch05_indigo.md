## 🌟 1. Overview & Strategy

> **Teacher's Direct Advice:**  
> 'Indigo' describes Mahatma Gandhi's first civil disobedience triumph in India (Champaran, Bihar, 1917):  
> 1. **Rajkumar Shukla's Tenacity:** An illiterate sharecropper from Champaran who relentlessly followed Gandhi to Lucknow, Kanpur, and Ahmedabad until Gandhi agreed to visit Patna and Champaran.  
> 2. **The Injustice:** British planters forced Indian sharecroppers to plant 15% (3/20th) of their holding with indigo and surrender the entire harvest as rent. When Germany developed synthetic indigo, planters extorted illegal compensation from peasants to release them from the 15% agreement.  
> 3. **The Turning Point of British Power:** When ordered to leave Champaran, Gandhi signed the summons with "disobey the order". Thousands of peasants spontaneously gathered around Motihari courthouse; the British government was paralyzed. Lieutenant Governor dropped the case.  
> 4. **The Settlement & Moral Victory:** Planters agreed to refund 25%. Gandhi accepted because the planters surrendered part of their prestige.  
> 5. **Self-Reliance (Charles Freer Andrews Episode):** Gandhi refused to take the help of an Englishman, teaching the lawyers that their cause was just and they must rely upon themselves.

## 📝 4. Solved Board Models (Step-by-Step with Marking Scheme)

### Model 1: Why did Gandhi agree to a settlement of merely 25 percent refund to the sharecroppers? (3 Marks)
**Answer:**  
1. **Prestige Over Money:** Gandhi explained that the actual amount of the refund was less important than the fact that the British landlords were obliged to surrender part of the money and, with it, part of their prestige and absolute authority. (1.5 Marks)  
2. **Breaking the Fear:** Heretofore, the British planters had behaved as lords above the law. The 25% refund shattered the myth of British invincibility and taught the Indian peasants that they had rights and defenders. (1.5 Marks)

## ⚡ 8. 1-Minute Quick Recap
- Champaran Sharecropping: 15% indigo arrangement (Tinkathia system)
- Rajkumar Shukla: Resolute peasant who brought Gandhi to Champaran
- First victory of Civil Disobedience in modern India: Motihari trial dropped
- Refund agreed: 25% (broke landlord prestige)
- Key lesson: Self-reliance (refused Charles Freer Andrews' assistance)
