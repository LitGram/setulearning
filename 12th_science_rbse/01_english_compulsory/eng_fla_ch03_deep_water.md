## 🌟 1. Overview & Strategy

> **Teacher's Direct Advice:**  
> 'Deep Water' is William Douglas's autobiographical account of conquering his childhood hydrophobia (fear of water). The 3 core exam themes:  
> 1. **The Genesis of Fear:** The California beach incident (waves knocking him down at age 3-4) and the misadventure at the YMCA pool (Yakima) where a big bruiser boy tossed 10-11 year old Douglas into the 9-foot deep end.  
> 2. **Near-Drowning Experience & Terror:** The nine feet felt like ninety; Douglas's three futile jump attempts; choking on water, paralyzing terror, and finally surrendering to gentle blackness/peace.  
> 3. **Systematic Rehabilitation & Final Triumph:** Hiring an instructor who built a swimmer "piece by piece" using a belt, pulley, and cable over seven months. Douglas tested his courage at Lake Wentworth (New Hampshire) and Warm Lake. Roosevelt's quote: *"All we have to fear is fear itself."*

## 📝 4. Solved Board Models (Step-by-Step with Marking Scheme)

### Model 1: How did the instructor turn William Douglas into a confident swimmer? (3 Marks)
**Answer:**  
1. **The Safety Gear:** The instructor put a belt around Douglas with an attached rope running through a pulley overhead. Douglas held the rope while swimming back and forth across the pool, practicing for hours and weeks without panic. (1 Mark)  
2. **Technique Training:** The instructor taught him to put his face underwater and exhale, and to raise his nose and inhale. He practiced this breathing technique hundreds of times. (1 Mark)  
3. **Leg Action & Integration:** Douglas was held at the side of the pool and made to kick with his legs for weeks until they relaxed and obeyed his will. Finally, after seven months, the instructor integrated these separate skills into a complete swimmer. (1 Mark)

---

## 🔑 9. Complete Explanatory Answer Key
1. The misadventure left a haunting fear in Douglas's heart. He avoided water for years; it ruined his fishing trips, boating, and canoeing. He developed an acute sense of deprivation until he decided to conquer it.  
2. President Roosevelt said this quote. In Douglas's case, death was peaceful; only the fear of death produced sheer terror. Once he conquered the psychological barrier of fear, he experienced true freedom.
