## Visual system

- Match the Biology series’ clean, high-contrast, zoomable teaching-graphic approach. Use Hindi-first labels with English names in parentheses where standard or useful; captions and short “चित्र से सीखें” notes should explain what to notice.
- Use precise editable diagrams, maps, timelines, comparison plates, flow charts and worked examples alongside polished representative illustrations where a scene helps students picture a place, practice or process. Scene illustrations must not be used to assert exact documentary details; pair them with a useful diagram or an explicit representative-image caption.
- Keep text and figures readable on a phone. Avoid dense labels inside outline maps; use numbered markers with a companion key. Use one consistent north arrow, scale convention, legend, and muted Rajasthan palette across maps.
- Label conceptual, stylized, reconstructed, or example-based artwork consistently as **“Representative illustration — for learning purposes; not a literal depiction.”** Hindi caption: **“प्रतिनिधि चित्रण — केवल शैक्षिक समझ हेतु; इसे वास्तविक दृश्य का हूबहू चित्र न मानें।”** Place the note in the caption or compact image note, not as a watermark over the graphic. For maps, diagrams, or charts that are meant to convey exact data, give their source/scale/date instead of applying this blanket note; if a map is schematic, label it “schematic / not to scale.”
- Reuse Rajasthan outline/map base among Books 1, 2, 5 and 6 where applicable; reuse India outline in Books 2–5. Do not repeat full maps where a compact locator inset is sufficient.
- Treat changing boundaries, administrative names, statistics, policies, budgets and current affairs as dated facts. Verify against the source book and an authoritative up-to-date reference immediately before asset production; include an “as of” date when a graphic could age.
- Historical images must be identified as representative reconstructions when evidence is incomplete and carry the representative-illustration note above. Do not invent labels, exact borders, dynastic associations, dates, proportions, or site locations. Check disputed historical interpretation against the book’s wording and reliable references.
